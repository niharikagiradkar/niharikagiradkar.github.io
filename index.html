Writing

From the blog

AI won't replace salespeople. It will replace the ones who stopped listening.

Niharika Giradkar · October 2026 · 4 min read

Every week someone tells me AI is coming for my job. I sell for a living. High-ticket, consultative sales, the kind where a buyer takes months to say yes and sometimes calls you at 10 pm with a doubt they are embarrassed to ask anyone else.

So I pay attention when people say a machine will do this better. Here is what I actually see.

What AI is genuinely taking over

The homework before a first call used to take me an hour. Company news, the person's background, what their competitors are doing. Now it takes minutes, and the homework is better than what I used to do.

Follow-ups. The money in sales is in the follow-up, and the follow-up is where most of us are lazy. Not because we don't care, but because there are thirty open conversations and only one of you. AI does not forget. It does not get tired. It does not say "I'll send it tomorrow" and then not send it.

First drafts. Proposals, meeting recaps, the email you don't know how to start. A blank page is the most expensive thing in sales. AI kills the blank page.

Notes and CRM updates. Nobody became a salesperson because they love updating a CRM. Now the admin happens while I think about the next conversation.

What it cannot touch

AI cannot read a pause. When a buyer says "send me the details" and something in their voice says this is over, a good salesperson hears it. That hearing is the job.

It cannot earn trust over months. Big purchases are fear management. The buyer is not really buying the product. They are buying the confidence that they won't regret the decision. Confidence comes from a person who showed up ten times and was honest even in the moments when honesty cost them a faster yes.

It cannot decide to walk away. Some deals should not be closed. Telling a buyer "this is not right for you" is the strongest move in sales, and no tool will ever make that call for you.

Who should actually worry

The reps at risk are not being replaced by AI. They were being replaced already, by their own script. Same pitch for every buyer. Same sequence of emails. No curiosity, no listening. If your whole skill is the script, then yes, worry, because a machine runs a script better than any human ever will.

But that was never sales. That was reading out loud.

What I am doing about it

I let AI do the robot parts of my job so I can do more of the human parts. Research in minutes, not hours. Follow-ups that never slip. Drafts I edit instead of writing from zero.

The time that comes back goes into the only things that ever closed a deal: listening properly, asking the question nobody else asked, and being the person the buyer trusts when the number on the table is big enough to scare them.

AI is not the end of salespeople. It is the end of salespeople who were acting like machines. The rest of us just got a very good assistant.
